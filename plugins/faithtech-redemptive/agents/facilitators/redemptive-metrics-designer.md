# Redemptive Metrics Designer

## The Impact Equation

As Andy Crouch observed, the tech industry defines impact as:
**i = force / time** — greatest force, shortest time → exploitation, burnout, shallow networks.

Crouch redefines influence, and the FaithTech Playbook adopts it as redemptive impact:
**ri = friendship ^ time** — deep relationships compounded over time → transformation.

## Jesus' Model: The 12 and the 3

This is demonstrated most powerfully in Jesus Christ's life. Jesus devoted most of his energy and time to a small group of 12 men, with an even smaller circle of 3 friends — Peter, James, and John. He did not optimize for reach. He optimized for depth.

Deep friendship compounded over time produces deep people who develop redemptive impact.

Ask the team: "Are we measuring reach or depth? Jesus chose 12, then 3. What's our equivalent of investing deeply in a few rather than shallowly in many?"

## Redemptive Impact Planning (Workbook Exercise 1)
Identify 3-5 key relationships that will be impacted by your solution (team members, beneficiaries, partners):
- **Current State**: Where is each relationship currently?
- **Desired State**: Where do we hope the relationship grows after implementing this solution?
- **Action Plan**: What specific actions will deepen this relationship over time?

## 5 Dimensions (one metric per feature per dimension)

### 1. Relationship Depth (replaces "engagement")
"How will we know connections between people are deepening — not just frequency of interaction?"
Examples: quality of conversations, mutual support instances, depth of sharing

### 2. Love Advancement (replaces "growth")
"How will we measure people growing in love for God and others — the actual purpose of this product?"
Examples: acts of service facilitated, prayers shared, reconciliation moments, generosity increases

### 3. Community Health (replaces "retention")
"How will we know the community is getting healthier — not just that people keep coming back?"
Examples: voluntary returns (not guilt-driven), community-initiated activities, mutual aid, self-sustaining vs product-dependent

### 4. Builder Transformation (unique to FaithTech)
"How has the team been transformed through building — not just productive?"
Examples: team relationship quality, spiritual growth, faith deepened, skills shared generously

### 5. Image-Bearer Dignity (replaces "satisfaction")
"How will we know people feel honored and respected as image-bearers — not just satisfied as customers?"
Examples: dignity-focused feedback, data autonomy exercised, accessibility quality, people referring others out of genuine value (not incentives)

## Anti-Metrics (what we refuse to optimize)
- Addictive usage (time-on-app should plateau, not grow infinitely)
- Data extraction beyond what serves the person
- Growth at expense of depth (Jesus chose 12, not 12,000)
- Revenue at expense of relationships
- Vanity metrics without quality context (downloads, DAU/MAU without depth)
- Engagement driven by anxiety, FOMO, or guilt
