# A Brief Theology of Technology

From the FaithTech Playbook (pp. 8-11). Read this to the team or offer as reflection before or during Phase 1: Discover.

## God's History with Technology — 8 Narratives

### 1. Hello World — The Creator Creates (Gen 1:1, 1:26)
God's first action was creating. He created us in his image and likeness to co-create with him. "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth and subdue it." We can create because God designed us to create. Our identity as technologists is as image-bearing co-creators with the Creator.

### 2. The First Upgrade — Sacrifice and Covering (Gen 3:7, 3:21)
Sin enters through deception. Our first parents covered shame with prototype clothes (fig leaves). God performed the first upgrade — sacrificing an animal to make real clothes. Humanity's natural posture is to cover brokenness with our own innovations. And upgrades come with a sacrificial price.

### 3. A Blueprint for Salvation — Noah's Ark (Gen 6:6, 6:14-16)
God redeemed civilization through a man, his family, and a technological marvel — an ark. God provided Noah with a detailed blueprint for an advanced technology to save humanity. Technology can be built for redemptive ends with God's intentions.

### 4. A New Invention: Bricks — Tower of Babel (Gen 11:3-6)
A new invention — bricks — gave way to building a tower to reach heaven. "Nothing they plan to do will be impossible for them." Humanity believed they could leverage technology to use God for their own benefit. This is the original Killer App Trap.

### 5. Craftsman for the Creator — Bezalel and the Tabernacle (Exod 31:2-5)
God "chose Bezalel and filled him with the Spirit of God, with wisdom, with understanding, with knowledge and with all kinds of skills." God chose skilled workers and gifted them with abilities to build the house of worship. Your technical gifts are Spirit-given for God's purposes.

### 6. The Cross Redeemed — Technology Transformed (Col 1:16)
The cross was a technological symbol of pain, intimidation, and rejection. Jesus takes it and redeems it into a symbol of hope, joy, and salvation. The Creator was rejected by his own creation — and redeemed the very tool of rejection.

### 7. Startup Church — Infrastructure for the Gospel (Acts, Pax Romana)
Advanced road systems, a common language, and common currency. The apostles were uniquely positioned to rapidly scale the Good News. God uses existing technological infrastructure for redemptive purposes.

### 8. The Holy City — Technology Redeemed (Rev 21:1-2)
"I saw the Holy City, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God." The city — a symbol of innovation, creativity, arts, and technology — God has redeemed and given his people to dwell in. Technology's final destiny is redemption, not destruction.

## Reflection Questions for Teams
- Which narrative resonates most with your project?
- Are you building an "ark" (redemptive blueprint from God) or a "tower" (reaching for God on your own terms)?
- How does knowing that Bezalel was Spirit-filled for technical work change how you view your own skills?
- What "cross" — a tool of harm — might your project redeem into something hopeful?

## Key Takeaway
God has a plan and purpose for technology and for our role in creating and redeeming it for his glory. Our responsibility is to create and leverage technology in such a way that leads to life and redemption, rather than abuse or idolatry.
